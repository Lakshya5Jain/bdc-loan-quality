BDC Loan Signal Book

How every number on the screen is built, from the SEC filing to the long and short lists, and what each one does and does not tell you. Written for review: every threshold here is the one in the code today.

Data through 30 Jun 2026 filings Prices through 9 Sep 2026 Public BDCs screened 48 of 54 Loan-quarters in the model 317,683 Backtest 15 quarters, Dec 2022 to Jun 2026

1What the system is

Every quarter each business development company (BDC) publishes its full schedule of investments: every loan it holds, what it paid for it (cost), what it says it is worth today (fair value), the rate, and whether it is still paying. The SEC forces this into machine-readable XBRL. We read all of it, for all 160 BDCs, every quarter since late 2022, and turn it into one table with one row per loan per quarter.

The trading idea is simple. A BDC's stock trades on its net asset value (NAV), and its NAV is the sum of these loan marks. Marks move loan by loan before NAV moves, and NAV moves before the market fully reprices the stock. Because many loans are held by several BDCs at once, we can also see which BDC is marking a shared loan more generously than its peers, which is a forward look at write-downs that have not happened yet.

01 ingest
SEC bulk XBRL files, monthly
02 holdings
one row per holding per quarter, subtotals and note tables removed, reconciled to the reported total
03 loans
the same loan linked across quarters into one loan id
04 loan signals
mark, mark change, non-accrual, PIK, amendments, peer comparison, risk score
05 BDC signals
book-level shares and trends, peer z-scores, quality score
06 market
price, NAV, price/NAV, returns, screen quadrants, long and short lists

Only the public BDCs are traded, but the whole universe is kept because the private ones hold the same loans and their marks are evidence.

2Source data and the trust gate

Tables: raw.num, raw.txt → core.reconciliation

Each filing's facts arrive as tagged numbers with a free-text identifier per holding, for example Icefall Parent, Inc. | First lien senior secured loan. There is no loan ID, no standard format, and filers tag subtotals, headings and note tables with the same tag as loans. A dozen public filers use a different scheme altogether, where a holding is a combination of axis members instead of a text identifier. Horizon, Kayne Anderson, Oxford Square, Palmer Square, Saratoga and TriplePoint are in that group.

The trust gate

For every BDC-quarter we sum the fair value of the rows we kept and divide by the total investments figure the BDC printed on its balance sheet. That ratio is coverage.

coverage = Σ fair_value(kept rows) / reported total investments at fair value data_ok = coverage between 0.90 and 1.10, or a documented override

Nothing downstream uses a quarter that fails the gate. Today 822 of 843 public BDC-quarters pass; for the June 2026 quarter, 48 of 48 screened names pass, and 40 of them sit within 2%.

The June 2026 quarter was also checked row by row against each filing's own inline XBRL: over 99% of holdings carry the exact fair value shown in the filing. The database is faithful to the filings. Where it is thin, it is because the filer tagged less.

Known source gaps. Maturity dates are absent for about fifteen BDCs, including Ares and Blue Owl, because they do not tag them. Non-accrual status comes from footnotes and only exists where the filer attached one, so non-accrual counts are a floor. Goldman Sachs BDC's bulk data drops 8-12% of its rows each quarter; those are read from the filing itself.

3From filings to loans

Tables: core.holdings → core.loans, core.loan_history

Each identifier is parsed into an issuer name (normalised to a borrower_key), an instrument type, and whether it is debt. Instrument types are first_lien, second_lien, subordinated, structured (CLO, JV, fund), preferred, equity, warrant, plus debt_other and equity_other when only the tagged facts (principal and rate, or shares) say which side it is.

Holdings are then linked quarter to quarter within a BDC into a stable loan_id, in this order, each stage only on rows the previous one left unmatched:

  1. Identical identifier text.
  2. Identifier with percentages and whitespace normalised (Goldman rewrites its percent-of-net-assets every quarter).
  3. Same issuer, instrument type, subtype and maturity, unique on both sides.
  4. Same issuer and instrument type, unique on both sides.
  5. Same issuer and debt/equity class, unique on both sides (catches filers that add "First Lien Debt" to their wording).
  6. Same issuer and class with several tranches on both sides: paired by closest cost, within 35%.
  7. Fuzzy issuer name (token-set ratio at least 92) with a unique instrument type.

Unmatched rows start a new loan. A loan that disappears is an exit: loss if its last mark was below 90, otherwise repaid_or_sold. Across the public universe 84% of holdings in a trusted quarter continue a loan from the previous trusted quarter.

Interpretation. New-loan and exit statistics are only as good as linking. A quarter where a filer changed its wording will show inflated "new" and "exited" counts. Check match_method before trusting turnover for a single name.

5Loan risk score

Tables: signals.loan_features, signals.signal_validation, signals.loan_risk

The score answers one question per loan: how likely is this loan to go bad within the next four quarters? "Bad" means it goes on non-accrual, is marked below 80, or exits at a loss. Loans that are already bad are excluded, and quarters whose four-quarter window has not fully elapsed are left unlabelled rather than assumed good.

Each binary feature gets a weight from our own history: the log of how much more often loans carrying it went bad than loans in general, capped at 3. The base rate today is 10.2% over 317,683 loan-quarters. The score is a logistic combination of the weights, shown 0-100.

weight(f) = clamp(ln( P(bad | f) / P(bad) ), 0, 3) if at least 30 loan-quarters carry f score = 100 · sigmoid( logit(base_rate) + Σ weight(f) )

Interpretation. The strongest predictor of a future loss is a mark that is already between 80 and 90: four in ten of those go on non-accrual or below 80 within a year. A 5-point markdown in one quarter, even from par, triples the odds. Peer signals matter: when another lender has the borrower on non-accrual, the loan this BDC still shows as performing goes bad twice as often as average. Spread increases carry no information on their own.

6BDC-level metrics

Tables: signals.bdc_quarter, signals.bdc_latest

All loan rows of a BDC-quarter are rolled up. Everything is cost-weighted, so a large loan counts more than a small one, and "share" means share of the debt book at cost.

total debt fair value / total debt cost. The average mark of the book.
pct_debt_below_95 / 90 / 80

nonaccrual_pct_cost / _fv

pik_share

new_deterioration_rate

Quality score

Each of nine components is turned into a z-score against every trusted BDC in the same calendar quarter, clipped to ±3, and averaged. The components are the share below 90, non-accrual share, PIK share, new deterioration rate, new non-accrual rate, and the four-quarter changes in share below 90, non-accrual share and PIK share, plus the four-quarter change in the book's mark. Higher means worse than peers. A BDC needs a trusted quarter, at least 10 loans and at least 3 components to get a score.

quality_score = mean over components c of clip( (x_c − mean_c) / sd_c , −3, 3 ) quality_trend_4q = quality_score − quality_score four quarters ago

Interpretation. A score near 0 is an average book. +0.5 is clearly worse than peers, −0.5 clearly better. The trend matters more than the level: a book going from −0.3 to +0.3 over four quarters is a book that is deteriorating faster than the sector, whatever its starting point.

7Cross-lender marks

Tables: signals.borrower_marks, signals.bdc_generosity, signals.lender_scorecard

As of June 2026, 3,986 borrowers appear in the books of two or more BDCs. For each such borrower, per instrument type, we compute every lender's mark and compare each one with the average of the others.

mark_vs_peers = my mark − average mark of the other lenders on the same borrower and instrument generosity = cost-weighted mean of mark_vs_peers over a BDC's shared loans

The scorecard adds three hindsight measures per BDC, on loans whose four-quarter window has closed:

bad_rate
share of its loan-quarters that went bad within a year. Underwriting quality.
late_mark_rate
of loans it carried at 97 or above, the share that went bad within a year anyway. Marks that did not warn.
early_warning_rate
of loans it put on non-accrual, the share already marked below 95 the quarter before. Honest marking.
loss_exit_rate
cost of loans exited below 90, over average debt cost.

8Market overlay and the screen

Tables: market.prices, market.nav, market.pnav_history → market.screen, signals.watchlist_stocks

Prices and dividends come from Yahoo Finance daily; NAV per share is the tagged balance-sheet figure from each filing. The screen joins the latest trusted quarter of each public BDC with:

p_nav
last close / latest NAV per share. The market's view of the book.
p_nav_pct, quality_pct, trend_pct, ret_6m_pct
percentile rank of each within the screened set, 0 to 1.
ret_3m / 6m / 12m
total return, dividends included.
nav_chg_4q
NAV per share change over four quarters.

A second, backtested score (validated_score, section 9) can also mark a side: short when it is at least +0.75 and p_nav is at or above median, long when it is at most −0.5 and p_nav is in the bottom 35%.

9Income and balance sheet

Table: signals.bdc_fundamentals, from the filing's own income statement and balance sheet

These are the metrics every BDC analyst starts with. They come from the same filings, tagged without a holding identifier, and were missing until this round.

nii, nii_ps
net investment income for the quarter and per share. Tagged by 53 of 53 screened names once the four filer variants are read.
div_coverage
NII per share / quarterly dividend rate, where the dividend rate is the trailing twelve months of cash dividends divided by four. Below 1.0 the dividend is not earned.
nii_roe
annualised NII / net assets. The return the book earns for shareholders before marks.
leverage
borrowings / net assets. Losses on a levered book hit NAV multiplied.
nav_chg_1q, nav_chg_4q

share_chg_4q

Share counts are checked against net assets / NAV per share and replaced by the implied count when the tagged figure is off by more than 25% (Prospect tags a different class).

9bThe return backtest

Tables: signals.bt_universe, bt_periods, bt_summary, bt_weights, bt_latest · command soi backtest

This is a point-in-time test on stock returns, not on NAV. Every quarter end gets one rebalance date 75 days later, by which time nearly every 10-Q and 10-K is in. A BDC enters the cross-section only if its filing was public by that date and the quarter passed the trust gate. Positions are taken at the close on the rebalance date and held to the next one; returns include dividends and are measured in excess of the equal-weight universe, so what is shown is the quality spread, not BDC beta.

Two statistics per signal per quarter: the rank correlation between the signal and the next quarter's excess return (IC), and the spread between the top and bottom quintile. Both are shown in the direction the signal is supposed to work, so positive is good.

Acting on the filing day is better than waiting. The rankings get steadier: the share below 90 ranked every one of 15 quarters correctly, with a t-statistic of 6.8 against 4.6 for the quarterly version. The default strategy

Average the peer-rank of four signals into one health score: share below 90, share below 95, book mark, and NAV per share change over four quarters. On each filing day, long the healthiest fifth, short the sickest fifth, equal weight, hold to the next filing. On liquid names only, this won 14 of 15 quarters with an average excess spread of 4.7% per hold and a worst quarter of −0.5%. Forty variations (one to seven signals, 5 to 12 names a side, equal or rank sizing) all work; this one is chosen for robustness, not because it was the best-looking line.

What this means. The level of stress in the marks is the trade. The share of debt below 90 ranked the next quarter correctly in 13 of 15 quarters and the composite in 9 of 11, including the Dec 2025 quarter when the whole sector fell 15% and the spread was still 12 points. The signal also works when the sector rallies (Mar 2024 and Mar 2026 were the two losing quarters, both strong up-quarters where the weakest names bounced hardest). Non-accruals, price to NAV, momentum and cross-lender generosity carry no information at the stock level in this sample. Dividend coverage runs slightly backwards: the names covering their dividend best have been the ones de-rating, which is worth understanding before using it either way.

Before trusting it. One credit cycle, 36 to 48 names per quarter, so a quintile is eight to ten stocks and a single blow-up moves the spread. BDCs acquired or delisted since 2022 are not in the universe, which flatters the short leg. The loan risk score is kept out of the composite because its weights were fitted on the whole sample. Borrow and financing costs are not modelled. Treat the annualised figures as the right order of magnitude, not a forecast.

10How to read the numbers

Read levels, then flows, then price

  1. Level of stress: pct_debt_below_90 and wavg_risk. Above 15% below 90 is a troubled book; below 5% is clean. This is the validated part.
  2. Flow of new stress: new_deterioration_rate and markdown_share. Above 3% of the book newly crossing 95 in a single quarter is a bad quarter; two in a row is a trend.
  3. Honesty of the marks: generosity, late_mark_rate, and the loan watchlists for loans this BDC carries near par that a peer marks below 90. This decides whether the NAV can be trusted.
  4. Price: p_nav against the sector median of 0.74 today, and the six-month return against the median of +3.8%. A worsening book at a premium is the short; a clean book in the bottom 30% of p_nav is the long.

Things that mislead

  • A non-accrual share of 0% usually means the filer did not tag non-accrual, not that there is none. Use marks instead.
  • PIK share is structural for venture lenders (Hercules 40%, TriplePoint 28%) and for BDCs with big preferred books. Compare within a peer group, not across.
  • Equity-heavy BDCs (Main Street, Capital Southwest, Gladstone Investment) have small debt books relative to NAV; their NAV is driven by equity marks the credit metrics do not cover.
  • A filer that changed its identifier format shows a spike in new loans and exits that quarter. Check match_method.
  • Generosity built on fewer than 40 shared loans, or the scorecard on fewer than 200 judged loan-quarters, is noise.
  • The June quarter's four-quarter window closes in mid 2027, so the loan model's most recent labels are from mid 2025. Recent quarters are scored with weights, not judged.

12Weak points to fix next

In order of how much each changes the trade.

  1. The screen's quadrant labels still use the hand-written rules. The backtested composite disagrees with them: it puts Goldman in the middle, not on the short list, and it ranks Hercules, Trinity and Main Street at the top despite their premiums. The screen should show the composite.
  2. Survivorship. BDCs acquired or delisted since 2022 (BlackRock Capital, Blue Owl III, Logan Ridge, First Eagle, Harvest) have no free price history; fixing this needs a paid price source.
  3. Timing, done: the filing-day version is tested and stronger (section 9b). Remaining: rebuild the walk-forward composite in the event framework and produce an equity curve with turnover and borrow costs.
  4. Shared-borrower lead-lag. Generosity does not predict at the stock level, but the loan-level result says peer marks do predict loan outcomes. The stock-level version should be "how much of this BDC's book has a peer marked lower", weighted by size, and it should be tested as an intra-quarter trigger.
  5. Peer groups. Venture lenders and equity-heavy BDCs are scored against first-lien lenders. Z-scores within peer groups would sharpen the marks signals.
  6. Costs and capacity. Borrow availability and cost per name, especially for the small names that dominate the bottom quintile.
  7. Dividend coverage runs backwards. Find out whether that is a real pattern (high coverage names were the ones cutting anyway) or a data artefact before it earns a weight.
